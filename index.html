The east slope

Mount Stuart closes the north end at 9,415 feet, the second-highest non-volcanic peak in Washington, and a mile of bare granite that fills the sky the moment you crest Longs Pass. The flagship colorway is named for it — stone heather and graphite, the two colors of that face in the afternoon.

Beneath it, Lake Ingalls sits in a bowl of rust-orange peridotite, ringed by larch that turn gold for two weeks every October. That is the accent color, and the only color on either sock that isn't earned by the rock.

The beauty and the difficulty are the same fact.

I grew up on this side of the crest and I have wrecked a lot of feet up here. The sock that finally worked cost me thirty-two dollars, and when I cut it apart there was nothing inside worth thirty-two dollars. So we built it properly, in the same gauge, and priced it honestly.

Scott Palmer · Founder · Ellensburg, WA
